# 4YOS Camp curriculum

Status: for your approval, 5 Nov 2026. It replaces the course list in `11-teaching-plan.md`.

The sequence is authored for 4YOS Camp. Institutional maps and international subject benchmarks help check breadth and depth. External materials support specific lessons, and employer evidence informs applied briefs alongside the academic work.

## 1. The size of it

| Year | Courses | Lessons |
|---|---|---|
| Year 1: Foundations | 18 | 436 |
| Year 2: Core | 13 | 330 |
| Year 3: Going deep | 10 | 259 |
| Year 4: Advanced and specialist | 12 | 257 |
| **Total** | **53** | **1282** |

**Every course gets full lessons.** That includes physics, general subjects, older languages and the small specialist security topics, which the earlier plan left out. This is the list of lesson titles. Each lesson is written to the standard in section 3 when its turn comes.

The table counts planned lessons. Use the master plan's coverage and release rules to distinguish planned, authored, reviewed and available material.

**Estimated time:** about 1,068 hours of lessons at 50 minutes each, plus about 612 hours of tests, exams and projects. Over 4 years of 44 weeks, that's about 10 hours a week. That's a lot on top of attending two schools. Much of it is the same learning your schools examine, so it isn't all extra. Today fits each day to your real time, and it's fine if this takes longer than 4 years. These are my estimates.

## 2. How to read this

- **Course:** starts from nothing about its subject, and uses only what earlier lessons taught.
- **Needs:** courses to finish first. The order was checked by a script, so no course depends on a later one.
- **Unit:** a group of lessons that ends in a unit test.
- **Every course ends** in a timed exam and a real project.
- Lessons marked **(lab)** are done for real with a tool, and **(practice)** have no new idea, only many problems. All others teach.
- The [course project register](#6-course-project-register) specifies the independent work and evidence for all 53 courses. The [master plan](14-master-plan.md#22-4yos-camp-academic-and-global-readiness-standard) defines assessment and release requirements.

## 3. What every lesson contains

1. Why it matters.
2. A short recall from earlier lessons.
3. A real lecture segment where a good one exists, checked against its transcript. Otherwise it says plainly that it's reading only.
4. A complete written lesson that works with no video or internet, assuming nothing that wasn't taught earlier.
5. Worked examples, line by line.
6. Stop-and-think questions.
7. Common mistakes.
8. Practice on paper, then on something real.
9. A check with new questions on every retry.
10. Flashcards, and new questions a week later.
