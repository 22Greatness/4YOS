# Goal
Convert between percentages, fractions and decimals, find a percentage of anything, work out percentage change, and undo a percentage correctly.

# Why
Interest, discounts, tax, exam scores, inflation and most statistics in the news are percentages, and a lot of them mislead people who can't check them. Getting these right protects your money. Being able to read them protects you from bad claims, and you'll need them throughout statistics.

# Warm-up
?? Write $\frac{3}{4}$ as a decimal.
=> 0.75.

?? What is $0.2 \times 350$?
=> 70.

# Lesson
## Percent means out of a hundred
**Percent** means "per hundred", and % is its symbol. So 35% means 35 out of 100, which is $\frac{35}{100}$. We use percentages because a shared bottom of 100 makes things easy to compare.

| Percentage | Fraction | Decimal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50% | $\frac{1}{2}$ | 0.5 |
| 25% | $\frac{1}{4}$ | 0.25 |
| 75% | $\frac{3}{4}$ | 0.75 |
| 10% | $\frac{1}{10}$ | 0.1 |
| 1% | $\frac{1}{100}$ | 0.01 |
| 100% | 1 | 1 |
| 150% | $\frac{3}{2}$ | 1.5 |

### Converting
| From | To | Do this | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Percentage | Decimal | Divide by 100 | $35\% = 0.35$ |
| Decimal | Percentage | Multiply by 100 | $0.075 = 7.5\%$ |
| Percentage | Fraction | Put over 100, and simplify | $35\% = \frac{35}{100} = \frac{7}{20}$ |
| Fraction | Percentage | Divide, then multiply by 100 | $\frac{3}{8} = 0.375 = 37.5\%$ |

A percentage can go above 100%. If something doubles, it's 200% of what it was.

## Percentages of an amount
"Of" means multiply. Change the percentage to a decimal, and multiply.

For 35% of ₦12,000:

$$0.35 \times 12{,}000 = 4{,}200$$

So it's ₦4,200.

### Doing it in your head
10% is one tenth, so just move the point. From that, you can build most others.

For 35% of ₦12,000: 10% is ₦1,200, so 30% is ₦3,600. 5% is half of 10%, which is ₦600. Together, they're ₦4,200.

### What percent is it?
To find what percentage one number is of another, divide the part by the whole, then multiply by 100.

You scored 42 out of 60: $\frac{42}{60} = 0.7 = 70\%$.

?? What is 15% of 240?
=> $0.15 \times 240 = 36$. Or 10% is 24, and 5% is 12, and $24 + 12 = 36$.

## Increase and decrease
To raise an amount by 20%, you could find 20% and add it. Faster is one multiplication. The new amount is 100% + 20% = 120% of the old one, so multiply by 1.2. To lower an amount by 20%, what's left is 80%, so multiply by 0.8.

| Change | Multiply by | ₦5,000 becomes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up 20% | 1.2 | ₦6,000 |
| Up 7.5% | 1.075 | ₦5,375 |
| Down 20% | 0.8 | ₦4,000 |
| Down 5% | 0.95 | ₦4,750 |

### Percentage change
To find how much something changed, use:

$$\text{percentage change} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \times 100$$

A price went from ₦800 to ₦1,000. The change is ₦200, and $\frac{200}{800} \times 100 = 25\%$ up. Always divide by the **old** value.

If it goes back from ₦1,000 to ₦800, that's $\frac{-200}{1000} \times 100 = -20\%$. Up by 25%, then down by 20%, gets you back to where you started, because the starting amounts differ.

> Mistake: Thinking that up 50% and then down 50% returns you to the start. ₦1,000 goes up to ₦1,500, then down to ₦750.

### Changes in a row
Each change applies to the latest amount, so multiply the factors. Up 10% twice is $1.1 \times 1.1 = 1.21$, which is up 21%, not 20%. That's how compound interest grows.

## Going backwards
A shirt costs ₦6,000 after a 20% discount. What was the original price? It's tempting to add 20% to ₦6,000, giving ₦7,200. That's wrong, because the 20% was of the original, not of ₦6,000.

Use the factor. Original × 0.8 = 6,000, so divide:

$$6{,}000 \div 0.8 = 7{,}500$$

The original price was ₦7,500.

Check: 20% of ₦7,500 is ₦1,500, and $7{,}500 - 1{,}500 = 6{,}000$.

> Mistake: Undoing a percentage with the same percentage in the other direction. To undo a multiplication, you divide by the same factor.

## Percentages that mislead
### Points and percent
If a rate goes from 10% to 12%, it rose by 2 **percentage points**. But it rose by 20% in relative terms, since $\frac{2}{10} = 20\%$. Both are correct, and they're very different statements. People choose whichever sounds better for their story.

### Ask: percent of what?
"Profits up 100%" may mean ₦1,000 went to ₦2,000. "90% of students passed" may mean 9 of a class of 10. A percentage hides the size of the group. Always ask what the percentage is of, and how big that is.

### Worked example: fees
A loan of ₦50,000 charges 5% per month. Over 3 months, with the interest added each time, you owe:

$$50{,}000 \times 1.05 \times 1.05 \times 1.05 = 57{,}881.25$$

That's ₦57,881.25, or ₦7,881.25 in interest.

This example's numbers are invented, but this is how to read any loan. Always work out what you'll actually pay.

# Exercises
?? Write 12.5% as a decimal and as a fraction.
=> 0.125 and $\frac{1}{8}$.

?? What percentage is 18 out of 40?
=> $18 \div 40 = 0.45 = 45\%$.

?? A ₦4,500 price goes up by 8%. What's the new price?
=> $4{,}500 \times 1.08 = 4{,}860$, so ₦4,860.

?? A bag goes from ₦12,000 to ₦9,000. What's the percentage change?
=> $\frac{-3{,}000}{12{,}000} \times 100 = -25\%$. A 25% drop.

?? A phone costs ₦115,000 including 15% tax. What's the price before tax?
=> $115{,}000 \div 1.15 = 100{,}000$, so ₦100,000.

2. Work out your real scores from any recent test as percentages, and compare two with different totals.
3. Find a percentage in a news story or advert. Ask: percent of what? How big is that? Write down what's missing.

# Summary
- Percent means out of 100. Divide by 100 to get a decimal.
- A percentage of an amount: change to a decimal and multiply.
- Up 20% means times 1.2, and down 20% means times 0.8.
- Percentage change is $\frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \times 100$.
- To undo a percentage, divide by the same factor.
- Points and percent differ. Always ask: percent of what?

# Check
Q: Write 7% as a decimal.
P: Percent means out of a hundred
A: 0.07 | .07
= Divide by 100. It isn't 0.7, which would be 70%.

Q: Write $\frac{3}{5}$ as a percentage.
P: Percent means out of a hundred
A: 60 | 60%
= $3 \div 5 = 0.6$, which is 60%.

Q: What is {p}0% of {a}00?
P: Percentages of an amount
Vars: p=1..9, a=1..9
F: p*a*10
= {p}0% is {p} tenths, so it's {p} × {a}00 ÷ 10 = {p*a*10}.

Q: You got 27 out of 30. What percentage is that?
P: Percentages of an amount
A: 90 | 90%
= $27 \div 30 = 0.9$.

Q: Which factor raises an amount by 6%?
P: Increase and decrease
- 0.06
- 1.6
* 1.06
= 100% + 6% = 106%, which is 1.06. 1.6 would be up 60%.

Q: Rent goes from ₦400,000 to ₦500,000. What's the percentage increase?
P: Increase and decrease
A: 25 | 25%
= $\frac{100{,}000}{400{,}000} \times 100 = 25\%$. Divide by the old value.

Q: ₦10,000 goes up by 50%, then down by 50%. What is it now, in naira?
P: Increase and decrease
A: 7500 | 7,500 | ₦7,500 | ₦7500
= $10{,}000 \times 1.5 \times 0.5 = 7{,}500$. The down step applies to a bigger amount.

Q: A shirt costs ₦4,000 after 20% off. What was the original price, in naira?
P: Going backwards
A: 5000 | 5,000 | ₦5,000 | ₦5000
= $4{,}000 \div 0.8 = 5{,}000$. Adding 20% of ₦4,000 would give ₦4,800, which is wrong.

Q[transfer]: A pass rate rises from 40% to 50%. A headline says it's "up 25%", and another says it's "up 10 points". Which is correct?
P: Percentages that mislead
- Only "up 25%", because percentages are always described in relative terms
- Only "up 10 points", because a rate can't go up by a percentage
* Both, because they measure the same rise in two different ways
= 50 − 40 = 10 points. Also $\frac{10}{40} = 25\%$. You should know which one you're being shown.

Q[transfer]: An offer: "Pay ₦20,000 now, and it grows 10% each month for 2 months." How much is that after 2 months, in naira?
P: Increase and decrease
A: 24200 | 24,200 | ₦24,200 | ₦24200
= $20{,}000 \times 1.1 \times 1.1 = 24{,}200$. It's compound growth, not ₦24,000. Treat real offers like this with care.

Q[transfer]: An advert claims, "9 in 10 students got better grades with this app." What's the first thing you should ask?
P: Percentages that mislead
* How many students were asked, and how they were chosen
- Whether nine in ten is the same thing as 90 percent
- Whether the grades were written down as whole numbers
= A percentage hides the size of the group. Ten chosen students prove nothing.
