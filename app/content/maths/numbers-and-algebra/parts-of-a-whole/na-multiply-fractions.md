# Goal
Multiply and divide fractions and mixed numbers, cancel before you multiply, and know why dividing by a fraction works the way it does.

# Why
"Half of a third" and "how many ₦250 cards can ₦1,500 buy?" are both this. Probability uses multiplication of fractions whenever two things must both happen. In algebra, you'll keep using the reciprocal to clear fractions and solve equations. Unlike adding, you don't need matching bottoms, so this is actually the easier half of fractions.

# Warm-up
?? What is $\frac{3}{5}$ of 40?
=> $40 \div 5 \times 3 = 24$.

?? Write $2\frac{1}{3}$ as an improper fraction.
=> $\frac{7}{3}$.

# Lesson
## Multiplying fractions
Multiply the tops, multiply the bottoms.

$$\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \times c}{b \times d}$$

Why? "Of" means multiply. What's $\frac{1}{2}$ of $\frac{1}{3}$? Cut a bar into 3 parts, then cut each part in half. Now there are 6 small parts, and half of one third is one of those: $\frac{1}{6}$. So $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1 \times 1}{2 \times 3} = \frac{1}{6}$.

### Worked example
$$\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{4}{5} = \frac{8}{15}$$

No common denominator is needed here. Don't use one, as it would just add work.

### Whole numbers
A whole number is a fraction over 1, so $5 \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{5}{1} \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{10}{3} = 3\frac{1}{3}$.

Notice that multiplying by a fraction smaller than 1 makes something **smaller**: $\frac{1}{2} \times 8 = 4$. "Multiplying makes bigger" is only true for numbers above 1.

### Mixed numbers
Change them to improper fractions first.

$$1\frac{1}{2} \times 2\frac{1}{3} = \frac{3}{2} \times \frac{7}{3} = \frac{21}{6} = \frac{7}{2} = 3\frac{1}{2}$$

> Mistake: Multiplying the wholes and the fractions separately. $1\frac{1}{2} \times 2\frac{1}{3}$ isn't $2\frac{1}{6}$. Each part of the first number has to multiply each part of the second.

## Cancelling first
Why multiply big numbers, then simplify? Divide out common factors before you multiply. A factor of any top can cancel with a factor of any bottom.

$$\frac{8}{15} \times \frac{5}{12}$$

8 and 12 share 4, leaving 2 and 3. 5 and 15 share 5, leaving 1 and 3.

$$\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{9}$$

Without cancelling you'd get $\frac{40}{180}$, then have to simplify that. The answer's the same, and the work is much bigger.

> Mistake: Cancelling two tops, or two bottoms. Cancelling works only across a top and a bottom, because that's what makes it a division by 1. It's also only for multiplying. In $\frac{2}{3} + \frac{3}{4}$, you can't cancel anything.

?? Work out $\frac{9}{14} \times \frac{7}{12}$, cancelling first.
=> 9 and 12 share 3, leaving 3 and 4. 7 and 14 share 7, leaving 1 and 2. So $\frac{3}{2} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{8}$.

## Dividing fractions
The **reciprocal** of a fraction that isn't zero is the fraction flipped upside down. Zero has no reciprocal, which is another way of saying you can't divide by it. The reciprocal of $\frac{2}{3}$ is $\frac{3}{2}$, and the reciprocal of 5 is $\frac{1}{5}$. A number times its reciprocal is always 1.

To divide by a fraction that isn't zero, multiply by its reciprocal.

$$\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c}$$

### Why flipping works
Division asks how many fit. How many halves fit into 3? Each whole holds 2 halves, so 3 wholes hold 6. That's $3 \div \frac{1}{2} = 3 \times 2 = 6$.

How many $\frac{2}{3}$ fit into 4? Each whole holds $\frac{3}{2}$ of them, one and a half, so 4 wholes hold $4 \times \frac{3}{2} = 6$. Check: six lots of $\frac{2}{3}$ is $\frac{12}{3} = 4$. Yes.

### Worked example
$$\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{5}{8} = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{8}{5} = \frac{3}{1} \times \frac{2}{5} = \frac{6}{5} = 1\frac{1}{5}$$

Only the second fraction flips. Only flip after you've changed any mixed numbers to improper fractions.

> Mistake: Flipping the first fraction, or both. In $\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d}$, you flip only what you're dividing by, $\frac{c}{d}$.

Dividing a positive number by a positive fraction below 1 gives a **bigger** answer. That's right: lots of small pieces fit into something.

## Signs and real problems
The sign rules from negative numbers still apply: $(-\frac{1}{2}) \times \frac{3}{5} = -\frac{3}{10}$, and $(-\frac{2}{3}) \div (-\frac{1}{3}) = 2$.

Read real problems for what they mean. "Of" means multiply. "How many fit" or "shared among" means divide.

| Problem | Calculation | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\frac{2}{5}$ of your ₦15,000 | $\frac{2}{5} \times 15{,}000$ | ₦6,000 |
| Half of $\frac{3}{4}$ of a loaf | $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{3}{4}$ | $\frac{3}{8}$ of a loaf |
| A $\frac{1}{4}$ hour task, in $2\frac{1}{2}$ hours | $\frac{5}{2} \div \frac{1}{4}$ | 10 tasks |
| $\frac{3}{4}$ of a tin shared by 6 | $\frac{3}{4} \div 6 = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{1}{6}$ | $\frac{1}{8}$ of a tin each |

# Exercises
?? Work out $\frac{3}{7} \times \frac{2}{5}$.
=> $\frac{6}{35}$.

?? Work out $\frac{4}{9} \times \frac{3}{8}$, cancelling first.
=> 4 and 8 share 4, leaving 1 and 2. 3 and 9 share 3, leaving 1 and 3. So $\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{6}$.

?? Work out $\frac{5}{6} \div \frac{2}{3}$.
=> $\frac{5}{6} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{5}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{4} = 1\frac{1}{4}$.

?? Work out $2\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{4}{5}$.
=> $\frac{5}{2} \times \frac{4}{5} = 2$, as both 5s cancel, and 4 and 2 share 2.

?? You've got 6 hours for a task that needs $\frac{3}{4}$ of an hour each time. How many times can you do it?
=> $6 \div \frac{3}{4} = 6 \times \frac{4}{3} = 8$.

# Practice
1. Think of something real you can take a fraction of, and then a fraction of that. For example, $\frac{1}{3}$ of your monthly money goes to food, and $\frac{1}{4}$ of the food money goes to rice. What fraction of your whole money goes to rice?
2. Think of a real "how many fit" question with a fraction. For example, how many $\frac{3}{4}$-hour study sessions fit into a free evening?
3. Work out both by hand, cancelling wherever you can.
4. Turn the first answer into naira, with a real or likely amount.

Write down both calculations and what the answers mean.

# Rubric
- I multiplied real fractions, cancelling first.
- I divided by a fraction, flipping only the one I'm dividing by.
- I can say what each answer means in real life.

# Summary
- To multiply: tops times tops, bottoms times bottoms. No common denominator is needed.
- "Of" means multiply.
- Cancel a factor from a top with one from a bottom before you multiply.
- To divide: multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction.
- Change mixed numbers to improper fractions first.
- Multiplying by a fraction below 1 makes it smaller, and dividing by one makes it bigger.

# Check
Q: What is $\frac{2}{5} \times \frac{3}{7}$, in the form a/b?
P: Multiplying fractions
A: 6/35
= Tops multiply to 6, and bottoms to 35.

Q: What is one {a}th of one {b}th? It's 1 over a number. Give that number.
P: Multiplying fractions
Vars: a=2..9, b=2..9
F: a*b
= Tops give 1, and bottoms give {a} × {b} = {a*b}.

Q: What is $\frac{3}{4}$ of $\frac{8}{9}$, in the form a/b?
P: Cancelling first
A: 2/3
= 3 and 9 share 3, leaving 1 and 3. 8 and 4 share 4, leaving 2 and 1. So $\frac{1}{1} \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{2}{3}$.

Q: Which is the reciprocal of $\frac{4}{7}$?
P: Dividing fractions
* $\frac{7}{4}$
- $-\frac{4}{7}$
- $\frac{4}{7}$
= The reciprocal is the fraction flipped upside down.

Q: What is $\frac{1}{2} \div \frac{1}{4}$?
P: Dividing fractions
A: 2 | two
= How many quarters fit in a half? $\frac{1}{2} \times 4 = 2$.

Q: What is $\frac{2}{3} \div \frac{5}{6}$, in the form a/b?
P: Dividing fractions
A: 4/5
= $\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{6}{5} = \frac{2}{1} \times \frac{2}{5} = \frac{4}{5}$.

Q: What is $1\frac{1}{3} \times 1\frac{1}{2}$?
P: Multiplying fractions
A: 2 | two
= $\frac{4}{3} \times \frac{3}{2} = 2$.

Q: A student cancels the 2 and 4 in $\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{4}$. What's wrong?
P: Cancelling first
- A 2 and a 4 don't share any common factor that can be divided out
* Cancelling only works for multiplying, and this is an addition
- Nothing is wrong, as cancelling works for all four operations
= With addition, use a common denominator. You can't cancel.

Q[transfer]: A coin lands on heads with a chance of $\frac{1}{2}$, and a die shows a 6 with a chance of $\frac{1}{6}$. For two independent events to both happen, you multiply. What's the chance of both, in the form a/b?
P: Multiplying fractions
A: 1/12
= $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{12}$. You'll use this in Probability.

Q[transfer]: A printer uses $\frac{2}{5}$ of a page's ink each minute. How long will $3\frac{1}{5}$ pages' worth of ink last, in minutes?
P: Signs and real problems
A: 8 | 8 minutes
= $\frac{16}{5} \div \frac{2}{5} = \frac{16}{5} \times \frac{5}{2} = 8$.

Q[transfer]: You divide 20 by $\frac{1}{2}$, and a friend expects an answer below 20. Who's right?
P: Dividing fractions
* You: the answer is 40, as 40 halves fit into 20
- Your friend: because division always gives a result below the start
- Your friend: because 20 divided by one half is the same as half of 20
= Half of 20 is $20 \times \frac{1}{2}$, which isn't $20 \div \frac{1}{2}$.

# Cards
How do you multiply fractions? :: Multiply the tops and multiply the bottoms. No common denominator is needed.
What does "of" mean in maths? :: Multiply. $\frac{1}{2}$ of $\frac{1}{3}$ is $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{3}$.
What's a reciprocal? :: The fraction flipped. A number times its reciprocal is 1.
How do you divide by a fraction? :: Multiply by its reciprocal. Flip only the second fraction.
Where does cancelling work? :: Between a top and a bottom, when multiplying only.
