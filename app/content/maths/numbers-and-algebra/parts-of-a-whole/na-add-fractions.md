# Goal
Add and subtract any fractions and mixed numbers, and give the answer in simplest form.

# Why
Real amounts rarely come in matching sizes. Half a term plus a quarter of a term, or adding probabilities in statistics, both need this. Later in this course, you'll add algebraic fractions by exactly these steps, so this is rehearsal for algebra as much as it's arithmetic.

# Warm-up
?? What is the LCM of 6 and 8?
=> 24.

?? Simplify $\frac{10}{12}$.
=> $\frac{5}{6}$.

# Lesson
## Same bottom
If the bottoms match, the parts are the same size, so you can just count them. Add or subtract the tops, and keep the bottom.

$$\frac{2}{7} + \frac{3}{7} = \frac{5}{7} \qquad\qquad \frac{6}{7} - \frac{2}{7} = \frac{4}{7}$$

Two sevenths plus three sevenths is five sevenths, just as 2 oranges plus 3 oranges is 5 oranges.

> Mistake: Adding the bottoms too. $\frac{2}{7} + \frac{3}{7}$ isn't $\frac{5}{14}$. Sevenths stay sevenths, because the size of the parts doesn't change when you count more of them.

Check that it's silly: $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}$ would give $\frac{2}{4}$, which says a half and a half make a half.

## Different bottoms
You can't add thirds to quarters directly, because the parts are different sizes. Rewrite them with a common denominator first.

### The four steps
1. Find the LCM of the bottoms.
2. Rewrite each fraction over that bottom.
3. Add or subtract the tops.
4. Simplify.

### Worked example: 3/4 + 5/6
The LCM of 4 and 6 is 12.

$$\frac{3}{4} + \frac{5}{6} = \frac{9}{12} + \frac{10}{12} = \frac{19}{12} = 1\frac{7}{12}$$

### Worked example: 7/10 − 1/4
The LCM of 10 and 4 is 20.

$$\frac{7}{10} - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{14}{20} - \frac{5}{20} = \frac{9}{20}$$

### Worked example: simplify at the end
$$\frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{6} + \frac{2}{6} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$$

Any common denominator works. Multiplying the two bottoms always gives one. The LCM just keeps the numbers small.

### Worked example: three fractions at once
The same steps work for any number of fractions. Find one bottom that all of them divide into, then rewrite each fraction over it. For $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4}$, the LCM of 2, 3 and 4 is 12, because 12 is the first number all three divide into exactly.

$$\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{6}{12} + \frac{4}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{13}{12} = 1\frac{1}{12}$$

To rewrite each one, ask what its bottom was multiplied by to reach 12, and multiply the top by the same number. For $\frac{1}{3}$, 3 was multiplied by 4 to reach 12, so the top becomes $1 \times 4 = 4$.

=> The LCM is 15. $\frac{10}{15} + \frac{3}{15} = \frac{13}{15}$.

## Mixed numbers
You've got two ways. The surest one is to turn everything into improper fractions, and work as usual.

### Worked example: 2 1/2 + 1 2/3
$$2\frac{1}{2} + 1\frac{2}{3} = \frac{5}{2} + \frac{5}{3} = \frac{15}{6} + \frac{10}{6} = \frac{25}{6} = 4\frac{1}{6}$$

### Worked example: 3 1/4 − 1 2/3
$$3\frac{1}{4} - 1\frac{2}{3} = \frac{13}{4} - \frac{5}{3} = \frac{39}{12} - \frac{20}{12} = \frac{19}{12} = 1\frac{7}{12}$$

The other way is to add the wholes and the fractions separately. That's quick for addition, but subtraction can get tricky when the second fraction is the bigger one, as above, where $\frac{1}{4} - \frac{2}{3}$ is negative. Improper fractions avoid that trouble.

### Whole numbers with fractions
A whole number is a fraction over 1. So $2 - \frac{3}{5} = \frac{10}{5} - \frac{3}{5} = \frac{7}{5} = 1\frac{2}{5}$.

## Checking and estimating
Fractions are easy to misjudge, so estimate first. Compare each one to 0, $\frac{1}{2}$ or 1.

$\frac{3}{4} + \frac{5}{6}$: both are close to 1, so the answer's a little under 2. We got $1\frac{7}{12}$, which fits. If you'd added the tops and bottoms and got $\frac{8}{10}$, the estimate would catch it straight away, as the result is smaller than one of the things you added.

Subtractions can be checked by adding back: $\frac{9}{20} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{9}{20} + \frac{5}{20} = \frac{14}{20} = \frac{7}{10}$, which is where we started.

### Real amounts
You spend $\frac{1}{4}$ of your money on transport and $\frac{1}{3}$ on food. What fraction is left?

$$1 - \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{12}{12} - \frac{3}{12} - \frac{4}{12} = \frac{5}{12}$$

If you've got ₦24,000, what's left is $\frac{5}{12}$ of it: $24{,}000 \div 12 \times 5 = 10{,}000$, so ₦10,000.

# Practice
1. Think of your real week, or your real money for a month. Choose three things that take up part of it. Give each one a fraction with a different bottom, roughly as it is for you.
2. Add the three fractions by hand with a common denominator. Do they come to less than 1, exactly 1, or more than 1? What would more than 1 mean in real life?
3. Work out the fraction left, and turn it into a real amount.
4. Estimate first using 0, $\frac{1}{2}$ and 1. Check your result against the estimate.

Write your fractions and what the result told you.

# Rubric

# Summary
- With the same bottom, add or subtract the tops and keep the bottom.
- With different bottoms, rewrite over a common denominator first, usually the LCM.
- Never add the bottoms.
- For mixed numbers, the surest way is to use improper fractions.
- Estimate with 0, $\frac{1}{2}$ and 1, and simplify at the end.

P: Different bottoms

P: Mixed numbers
A: 3 3/4 | 3 and 3/4
= $\frac{6}{4} + \frac{9}{4} = \frac{15}{4} = 3\frac{3}{4}$.

Q: What is $2 - \frac{2}{3}$, as an improper fraction in the form a/b?
P: Mixed numbers
A: 4/3
= $\frac{6}{3} - \frac{2}{3} = \frac{4}{3}$.

Q: A student works out $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{2}{6}$. Which quick check shows it's wrong?
P: Checking and estimating
- Two sixths has a bigger bottom than either of the two starting fractions
* $\frac{2}{6}$ is smaller than $\frac{1}{2}$, one of the things being added
- The tops were added, and adding tops is never allowed with any fractions
= Adding a positive amount must give more than you started with. The right answer is $\frac{3}{4}$.

Q[transfer]: You give $\frac{1}{3}$ of your ₦18,000 to family, and $\frac{1}{6}$ to savings. How much is left, in naira?
P: Checking and estimating
A: 9000 | 9,000 | ₦9,000 | ₦9000
= $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$, leaving half of ₦18,000.

Q[transfer]: A die shows 1 with probability $\frac{1}{6}$ and an even number with probability $\frac{1}{2}$. These can't happen together, so you add them. What's the probability of one or the other, in simplest form a/b?
P: Different bottoms
A: 2/3
= $\frac{1}{6} + \frac{3}{6} = \frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3}$. Adding fractions is how you combine probabilities like these.

Q[transfer]: Three study subjects take $\frac{1}{2}$, $\frac{1}{3}$ and $\frac{1}{4}$ of your week. What's wrong with this plan?
P: Checking and estimating
- Nothing, because these are all proper fractions that are smaller than 1
* They add to more than 1, so it's over the time you have
- Fractions with different bottoms can't be used for planning any week
= $\frac{6}{12} + \frac{4}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{13}{12}$, which is more than the whole week.

# Cards
How do you add fractions with the same bottom? :: Add the tops, and keep the bottom.
Why not add the bottoms? :: The bottom gives the size of the parts, and that doesn't change when you count more parts.
What are the steps for different bottoms? :: Find the LCM, rewrite both, add or subtract the tops, then simplify.
What's the surest way with mixed numbers? :: Change them to improper fractions first.
How do you write a whole number as a fraction? :: Over 1, or over whatever bottom you need: $2 = \frac{10}{5}$.
